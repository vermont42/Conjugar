^Purpose^

In Spanish, conjugation, a concept defined in %terminology%, is important for the reasons stated there. People raised in Spanish-speaking countries learn conjugation passively, and people who engage in formal study of Spanish learn conjugation as part of that study.

But there exists another category of Spanish-learners: people, like the developer of ~Conjugar~, who live in non-Spanish-speaking countries, who are currently unable to engage in formal study of Spanish, but who are exposed to Spanish vocabulary by one means or another. Here is an example of the challenge that these learners face.

The developer of ~Conjugar~ knew, from exposure to Spanish in a previous job, that "ir" means "to go" and that "ir" has certain conjugations starting with "va-". But this knowledge did not allow him to communicate with Spanish-speakers he encountered. For instance, to express the thought "I'm going to the office", he might have said "ir a la oficina" or "va a la oficina". But these sentences were nonsensical to Spanish-speakers because the conjugations were incorrect. The correct (and understandable) sentence was (and is) "$voY$ a la oficina".

There are excellent resources for Spanish-verb conjugation. The website ~SpanishDict.com~ and the book ~Spanish Verbs Made Simple(r)~ are two of them. The developer of ~Conjugar~ used these resources when he wrote Spanish, but they were unavailable when he spoke Spanish extemporaneously. At some point, he realized that he needed a way to practice conjugating Spanish verbs so that correct conjugations sprang to mind when he was speaking.

This was the genesis of ~Conjugar~, the app whose majesty now envelops you. ~Conjugar~ conjugates 4,811 Spanish verbs in every tense and lets you practice those conjugations, via quizzes, so that correct conjugations are in your brain at the exact moment you need them. ~Conjugar~ gamifies this experience by scoring each practice session and potentially uploading it to Game Center, allowing you to compete against the global Spanish-verb-conjugation community. Some tenses are rare except in archaic or formal texts and are less useful for many learners. ~Conjugar~ therefore provides three difficulty levels for practice sessions. ~Easy~ tests the three most important tenses. ~Moderate~ tests all tenses that Spanish-speakers commonly use. ~Difficult~ tests ~all~ tenses, including a few that you are unlikely to encounter unless you are reading, for example, ~El ingenioso hidalgo don Quixote de la Mancha~.

Conjugation is not the app's only ambition. ~Conjugar~ also explains ~why~ verbs behave as they do. Every verb belongs to a conjugation ~model~, a pattern shared with dozens or hundreds of other verbs, and the ~Models~ tab lets you study those patterns directly. Most verb screens show the verb's etymology and an example of the verb in use, drawn from Spanish literature and, for many verbs, from Medieval Spanish texts as old as the ~Cantar de mio Cid~. On devices that support Apple Intelligence, a conjugation tutor will answer your questions in conversation. And when you have had enough of the subjunctive, there is a game.

^Use^

Primary navigation in ~Conjugar~ occurs via the tab bar at the bottom of the screen: ~Browse~, ~Models~, ~Quiz~, ~Info~, and ~Settings~. Descriptions of these tabs follow. ~Conjugar~ runs on both iPhone and iPad; on the larger screen, lists and conjugation tables flow into multiple columns, but everything described here works the same way.

The first time you launch ~Conjugar~, a brief welcome tour introduces these tabs. You can dismiss it at any time and revisit it later by tapping ~Show Onboarding~ on the ~Settings~ tab.

^Browse^

~Conjugar~ starts on the ~Browse~ tab. This tab lists 4,811 Spanish verbs with their English translations. The most common verbs also show their frequency ranks; "ser", the most commonly used verb, is #1. Sort the verbs by frequency or alphabetically by tapping the control above the tab bar. To find a specific verb, type part of the Spanish infinitive ~or~ part of the English translation in the search field above the list. Search ignores accents and capitalization, so "esta" finds "estar" and "have" finds "haber" and "tener".

When you find a verb you would like to know more about, tap it. ~Conjugar~ then displays a screen with ~all~ conjugations of that verb. This screen is helpful for learning because it calls out, by highlighting in $RED$, parts of conjugations that are irregular. For example, the %presente de indicativo%, first-person singular conjugation of "dar" has an irregular "y", so ~Conjugar~ displays this conjugation as "$doY$". Tap any conjugation to hear it spoken.

Below the verb's translation are labels describing the verb. A verb is either regular (~Regular AR~, ~Regular ER~, or ~Regular IR~) or irregular, in which case the label names the model verb whose pattern it follows: "reconocer", for instance, is irregular and follows "conocer". Verbs that lack certain conjugations entirely, like "abolir", also carry a ~Defective~ label. Beneath these labels are the verb's %participio%, %gerundio%, and %raíz futura%, the three building blocks from which many other conjugations are formed.

Scroll past the conjugations and, for many verbs, you will find two more cards. The first gives the verb's ~etymology~: where the word came from, usually Latin, and sometimes Arabic, Gothic, or Greek. The second gives one or two ~example uses~. The first example is a sentence from Spanish-language literature or an official Spanish-language text, with an English translation and an attribution. The second, present for many common verbs, is a ~Medieval Spanish~ attestation, showing the verb as it was written centuries ago, with a translation. When a verb has several medieval attestations, tap the arrow to cycle through them. Sources for all of this material are listed in %credits%.

^Models^

Spanish verbs are less chaotic than they first appear. Nearly all of them conjugate according to one of 102 ~models~: patterns of stem changes and endings shared by a whole family of verbs. Learn "pedir", and you have most of "medir", "repetir", "servir", and a score of others.

The ~Models~ tab lists every model. Each row names the model's exemplar verb, the model's class number, and a badge showing how irregular the model is, expressed as the share of its conjugations that deviate from the regular pattern. The badge is green for models that are effectively regular, yellow for moderately irregular ones, and red for the truly wayward. Sort the list by irregularity, alphabetically, or by class number using the control above the tab bar, and search it by exemplar or class number.

Tap a model to see a compact grid of its conjugations, pronoun by tense, with irregular parts highlighted in $RED$ as elsewhere in the app. Scroll the grid horizontally to see every person. Below the grid is a list of every verb that follows the model; tap any of them to open its full verb screen.

^Quiz^

Tap ~Quiz~ in the tab bar to start a quiz. Tap ~Start~. ~Conjugar~ will then present you a set of fifty Spanish verbs to conjugate. Conjugate.

Each question shows the verb, its English translation, and the pronoun and tense you must produce. Type the conjugation and tap the keyboard's ~Next~ key. ~Conjugar~ tells you immediately whether you were right, showing the correct conjugation whenever you were not. A progress bar, your running score, and elapsed time appear on the screen throughout. To abandon a quiz, tap ~Quit~ in the upper-left corner.

If a letter in a word like "habló" requires an accent mark and you input, for example, "hablo", you will receive partial credit for that conjugation. To input an accented letter, hold your finger on ("long press") the letter you want to accent, "o" in this example, swipe to the accented letter, "ó" in this example, and release.

When you are done with the quiz, ~Conjugar~ will report and score your results, uploading the score to Game Center if it represents a personal best. The results screen lists all fifty questions with your answer and, where you erred, the correct conjugation, so a quiz doubles as a study session. Tap ~Quiz~ in the top-left corner to return to the ~Quiz~ tab. You can then start a new quiz.

^Info^

Tap ~Info~ in the tab bar to access information about ~Conjugar~, %terminology%, and the tenses. The ~Tenses~ section has a filter at its top for narrowing the list to the tenses quizzed in a given difficulty mode. The three options are ~Easy~; ~Easy~ and ~Moderate~; and ~Easy~, ~Moderate~, and ~Difficult~.

At the top of this tab, on devices that support it, is ~Conjugar~'s ~Conjugation Tutor~. The tutor is a chat: ask it to conjugate a verb, to explain why "tener" is irregular, or to compare the %pretérito% and the %imperfecto de indicativo%, and it will answer. Tap ~Get a Sample Question~ if you would like a suggestion to start with.

Two things about the tutor are worth knowing. First, it is ~grounded~ in ~Conjugar~'s own conjugation engine: when the tutor states a conjugation, it has looked that conjugation up in the same engine that produces the ~Browse~ tab's tables rather than inventing it. Second, it runs ~entirely on your device~, using Apple Intelligence. Your questions are not sent to ~Conjugar~'s developer, to Apple, or to anyone else. Because the tutor is a small on-device model, its answers can occasionally be incomplete or unexpected; the conjugations themselves, however, come from the engine.

The tutor requires an iPhone or iPad that supports Apple Intelligence, with Apple Intelligence turned on in the device's Settings app. When it is unavailable, the ~Info~ tab explains why, and, if Apple Intelligence is merely switched off, tapping the explanation opens Settings.

^Settings^

Tap ~Settings~ in the tab bar to access settings.

~Region.~ One class of conjugations, those of ~vosotros~, is important in Spain but largely absent in Latin America. If your goal is to communicate with Latin-American Spanish-speakers, you may want to have ~Conjugar~ omit ~vosotros~ conjugations from quizzes. If so, tap ~Latin America~ in the ~Region~ section. This setting also determines the accent in which ~Conjugar~ speaks conjugations aloud. In recognition of the added difficulty of learning ~vosotros~ conjugations, ~Conjugar~ assigns higher scores to quizzes that include these conjugations.

~Difficulty.~ Spanish verb tenses may be roughly grouped into three classes. The first class consists of the bare minimum of tenses you will need to express yourself in Spanish. If your goal is only to express yourself in Spanish, tap ~Easy~ in the ~Difficulty~ section. ~Conjugar~ will quiz you on these tenses. The second class consists of the tenses that native Spanish speakers regularly use in conversation and writing. If your goal is to understand spoken-and-written Spanish, tap ~Moderate~ in the ~Difficulty~ section. ~Conjugar~ will quiz you on these tenses. The third class consists of all the Spanish verb tenses, including a few that are considered archaic or are rarely used except in formal writing. If your goal is to understand archaic-or-formal Spanish texts, tap ~Difficult~ in the ~Difficulty~ section. ~Conjugar~ will quiz you on ~all~ Spanish verb tenses. In recognition of the relative difficulties of learning to conjugate the ~Easy~, ~Moderate~, and ~Difficult~ classes of verbs, ~Conjugar~ assigns higher scores to ~Moderate~ quizzes than to ~Easy~ quizzes and assigns higher scores to ~Difficult~ quizzes than to ~Moderate~ quizzes.

~Tú and vos.~ As described elsewhere in ~Conjugar~, %voseo% is a phenomenon whereby certain Spanish speakers use the pronoun ~vos~ and its conjugations instead of ~tú~ and its conjugations. In the ~Browse~ section, tap ~Vos~ to see ~vos~ conjugations instead of ~tú~ conjugations on the ~Browse~ and ~Models~ tabs. Tap ~Both~ to see both. In the ~Quiz~ section, tap ~Vos~ to be quizzed on ~vos~, rather than ~tú~, conjugations.

~App Icon.~ ~Conjugar~ ships with four Home Screen icons: the flamenco ~Dancer~, the ~Bull~, the ~Matador~, and the ~Classic~ icon the app wore for its first several years. Tap one to change it.

~The rest.~ The remaining sections launch ~Conjugar~'s game, replay the welcome tour, connect the app to Game Center if you have not already done so, and open the App Store, where you can rate or review ~Conjugar~. The app's version number appears at the bottom of the tab.

^Toreo por Amor^

~Conjugar~ contains a built-in, family-friendly game, ~Toreo por Amor~, reached from the ~Settings~ tab. You play a plucky flamenco dancer whose matador has been carried off by a bull. Climb five escalating stages of girders, dodging rolling hazards, grabbing power-ups like the smash-happy muleta, and surviving the occasional zombie stampede or blackout. Reach the top of the final stage, and a flamenco dance-off with the bull determines whether love conquers all. There is no way to lose: at worst, you begin the current stage again.

The game has nothing to do with Spanish-verb conjugation. It is there because the developer wanted to create it.

^Beyond the App^

~Conjugar~ also appears outside its own screens.

~Verb of the Day~ is a Home Screen and Lock Screen widget showing a different Spanish verb each day, with its conjugations. ~Daily Quiz~ is a Home Screen widget posing one conjugation question that you answer without opening the app. Add either from the Home Screen's widget gallery.

~Quick Quiz~ and ~Random Verb~ are Control Center controls. Add them to Control Center, or to the Lock Screen or Action Button, to start a quiz or open a random verb in one tap.

While a quiz is in progress, ~Conjugar~ posts a Live Activity showing your progress and score on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, so you can leave the app mid-quiz and come back to it.

^Accessibility^

~Conjugar~ supports Dynamic Type, so every screen respects the text size you have chosen in the device's Settings app, and it adapts to Dark Mode. It is designed for VoiceOver: conjugation tables, quiz feedback, and the game's controls are all labeled. Animations that pulse or breathe are suppressed when Reduce Motion is on. Any conjugation, example sentence, or medieval quotation can be tapped to hear it spoken aloud.

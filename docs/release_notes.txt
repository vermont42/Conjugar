3.0

Conjugar 3.0 is a rebuild. Every screen is new, the conjugation engine underneath is new, and the app now conjugates more than twenty times as many verbs as it did before.

A NEW CONJUGATION ENGINE
Conjugar now conjugates 4,811 verbs, up from about 200, in all 20 conjugated tenses. Browse or search the whole list, sorted by frequency or alphabetically.

MODELS
A new Models tab sorts the language into 102 conjugation models. Open one to see its pattern, its model verb, and every verb that follows it.

WHERE THE WORDS CAME FROM
Verb screens now include etymologies for more than 1,300 verbs, an example sentence for more than 1,300 verbs, each with an English translation, and more than 2,100 quotations from medieval Spanish texts, including the Poema de mio Cid and the Libro de buen amor.

CONJUGATION TUTOR
Ask a Spanish conjugation tutor how a tense works or how a verb is conjugated. It runs entirely on your device, and its answers are grounded in Conjugar's own conjugation engine. Nothing you type leaves your iPhone or iPad. Requires a device with Apple Intelligence enabled.

WIDGETS AND CONTROLS
Verb of the Day on the Home Screen and the Lock Screen. A Daily Quiz widget you can answer without opening the app. Control Center buttons for a quick quiz or a random verb. A Live Activity that follows a quiz in progress.

TOREO POR AMOR
Conjugar now hosts an arcade game: a five-stage climb through a Spanish festival, ending in a flamenco dance-off with a bull.

BUILT FOR iPAD
Conjugar is a native iPad app for the first time, with layouts made for the larger screen, rather than an iPhone app shown in a box.

REDESIGNED
Every screen was rebuilt in SwiftUI. Conjugar now supports Dark Mode, Dynamic Type, and VoiceOver, and offers four app icons to choose from. A welcome tour introduces the app on first launch and can be replayed from Settings.

QUIZ
Quizzes are now 30 questions rather than 50, a length people actually finish, and the three difficulty levels were rebalanced around it. Scores are scaled to the old 50-question range, so your existing Game Center leaderboard entries remain comparable.

ARTICLES
A new article traces the history of the Spanish verb system from Latin to today, the Purpose and Use article was rewritten, and the article list can be filtered by difficulty.

SPANISH
Everything new in this release is fully localized in Spanish, including the verb history, the etymologies, the medieval quotations, the tutor, the game, and the widgets.

Conjugar 3.0 requires iOS 26.

3.1

Conjugar 3.1 gives every verb a frequency rank and corrects the ranks that were wrong.

A RANK FOR EVERY VERB
Conjugar 3.0 ranked only the 988 most common verbs. Version 3.1 ranks all 4,811, from ser at #1 to presintonizar at #4,811. Every verb screen now shows a rank, and sorting the Verbs tab by frequency now orders the whole list, rather than the top thousand followed by everything else alphabetically.

BETTER DATA
The ranks now come from the Real Academia Española's CORPES XXI, a corpus of 455 million words of 21st-century Spanish from every Spanish-speaking country, which the RAE publishes under a Creative Commons license. Ties are broken with Google Books data. The previous ranks came from a web corpus whose tagger mistook common nouns for rare verbs, so hacendar, paginar, visar, and salar ranked among the 1,000 most common verbs on the strength of hacienda, página, visa, and sala. They now sit where they belong, and everyday verbs such as mirar, sentir, and oír have moved up. The Credits screen describes the sources.

TWO VERBS CORRECTED
Two entries in the verb list were not verbs. The misspelling reeligir is now reelegir, and sobre(e)ntender, a dictionary's shorthand for two spellings, is now sobrentender.

Spanish

Conjugar 3.1 da a todos los verbos un rango de frecuencia y corrige los rangos que estaban mal.

UN RANGO PARA CADA VERBO
Conjugar 3.0 clasificaba solo los 988 verbos más frecuentes. La versión 3.1 clasifica los 4.811, desde ser en el puesto 1 hasta presintonizar en el 4.811. Todas las pantallas de verbo muestran ahora su rango, y ordenar la pestaña Verbos por frecuencia ordena ahora toda la lista, en lugar de los mil primeros seguidos del resto en orden alfabético.

MEJORES DATOS
Los rangos proceden ahora del CORPES XXI de la Real Academia Española, un corpus de 455 millones de palabras del español del siglo XXI de todos los países hispanohablantes, que la RAE publica bajo una licencia Creative Commons. Los empates se resuelven con datos de Google Books. Los rangos anteriores venían de un corpus web cuyo etiquetador confundía sustantivos comunes con verbos raros, de modo que hacendar, paginar, visar y salar figuraban entre los 1.000 verbos más frecuentes gracias a hacienda, página, visa y sala. Ahora ocupan el lugar que les corresponde, y verbos cotidianos como mirar, sentir y oír han subido. La pantalla de créditos describe las fuentes.

DOS VERBOS CORREGIDOS
Dos entradas de la lista no eran verbos. La falta de ortografía reeligir es ahora reelegir, y sobre(e)ntender, la abreviatura de un diccionario para dos grafías, es ahora sobrentender.

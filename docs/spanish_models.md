# Spanish Verb Models

Transcribed from **_Spanish Verbs Made Simple(r)_**, Annex A — "Models of Verb
Classes and Sub-classes" (book pages 167–214, PDF pages 180–227 of
`spanish_verbs_made_simpler.pdf`).

These 35 classes (with their sub-classes) are the parsimonious model set that
this project aims to bring to Conjugar, mirroring how Conjuguer represents
French verbs.

## Conventions used in this file

- **Person order** in every table: `yo`, `tú`, `él` (3s), `nosotros` (1p),
  `vosotros` (2p), `ellos` (3p).
- **Tenses.** Indicative: Present, Simple Past (preterite), Imperfect, Future,
  Conditional. Subjunctive: Present, Imperfect (I) = the `-ra` form,
  Imperfect (II) = the `-se` form. Plus Imperative (2s / 2p = tú / vosotros),
  Past Participle, Present Participle.
- **Abbreviated sub-class tables.** For most sub-classes the book shows only the
  tenses that differ from the parent class model. A **blank cell means "same as
  the parent model."** When any single form in a tense differs, the book prints
  that whole tense column, so the full column is reproduced here.
- **`—`** means *no such form exists* (defective verbs, e.g. `abolir`).
- **Marker symbols** `*`, `+`, `†` after a form reproduce the book's footnote
  markers; they flag which orthographic/irregular rule named in that model's
  header produces the form.
- The book italicizes/bolds stem-stressed and irregular forms as a teaching aid;
  that per-form styling is **not** reproduced here (the forms themselves are
  faithful).
- Italicized sub-classes in the index (e.g. `2-6 romper`) are identical to their
  class model apart from an irregular past participle.

### General notes from the book

For the sub-classes, in general only tenses where one or more conjugations
differ from the class model are shown; for **2-3** (leer), **3-14** (abolir),
**6A-1** (erguir), **6B-1** (elegir), **6B-2** (seguir), **6B-3** (ceñir),
**6B-4** (reír) and **14-1** (prever) the full conjugations are shown.

For all verbs apart from *ser*, *haber*, and *ir*, the relevant irregularities
and/or orthographic modifications are presented in summary form at the top of
each model. Note that:

1. For a number of verbs, changes occur in conjugations in which the stress
   accent falls on the stem (root) syllable: present indicative (1s/2s/3s/3p),
   present subjunctive (1s/2s/3s/3p), and imperative (2s).
2. An irregular first person singular present indicative always implies an
   irregular present subjunctive.
3. An irregular simple past implies a (precisely) corresponding irregular
   imperfect subjunctive.

# Voseo (supplement — not from the book)

> **Provenance.** *Spanish Verbs Made Simple(r)* is peninsular and contains **no
> voseo**; everything above this line is the verified transcription of the book.
> This section is a deliberate, separately-sourced **addition** so the engine has
> a gateable `vos` truth. Its forms follow the RAE (*Nueva gramática*, §16.15f)
> and also match the **shipping Conjugar app's** existing `vos` behavior (the
> `svpr`/`svio` slots in `Conjugar.mig/Conjugar/verbs.xml`) — so it doubles as the
> differential-oracle reference for `vos`. It is **not** part of the verified
> book transcription and should not be counted as such.

## The rule

Voseo (rioplatense / general American `vos`) differs from `tú` in **exactly two
slots**. In **every other tense** `vos` uses the **`tú`** form (vos cantabas, vos
cantaste, vos cantarás, vos cantarías, que vos cantes, …).

1. **Presente de indicativo, 2s** — regular stem + **`-ás` / `-és` / `-ís`**,
   **bypassing** any diphthong or `-ir` raising. So the present-tense stem-vowel
   features (`d-ie`, `d-ue`, `r-ei-str`, …) **do not apply** here:
   `vos pensás` (not *piensás*), `vos tenés` (not *tienes*), `vos dormís`,
   `vos pedís`. This is the key engine rule: the `vos` present-2s slot is computed
   from the **regular** stem, immune to STR stem-vowel changes.
2. **Imperativo afirmativo, 2s** — **infinitive − `r`, final vowel accented**:
   `cantá / comé / subí`. Regular for essentially every verb, including those with
   an irregular `tú` imperative (`tené`, not *ten*; `poné`; `hacé`; `decí`; `salí`).

Present **subjunctive** 2s takes the **`tú`** form (`cantes / comas / subas`); the
voseo subjunctive (`cantés`, `comás`, …) is a regional variant and is **out of
scope** (the shipping app stores no `vos` subjunctive, and neither will the engine
for now).

## Exemplars (the three regular roots)

| Root | Pres. Ind. 2s (vos) | Imperative 2s (vos) |
|---|---|---|
| cantar (-ar) | vos **cantás** | **cantá** |
| comer (-er) | vos **comés** | **comé** |
| subir (-ir) | vos **subís** | **subí** |

(For `-ir`, vos and vosotros coincide in the present: `vos subís` = `vosotros subís`.)

## Irregular voseo forms

Only one verb has an irregular `vos` **present**, and one has an irregular `vos`
**imperative**; all other irregularity is handled by the two rules above.

| Verb | Pres. Ind. 2s (vos) | Imperative 2s (vos) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ser | vos **sos** | **sé** | the sole present-tense voseo irregular |
| ir | vos **vas** (= tú) | **andá** | present regular; imperative suppletive (book/app form) |

`haber → vos has`, `dar → vos das`, `estar → vos estás`, `ver → vos ves` are all
identical to the `tú` form and need no special handling.
